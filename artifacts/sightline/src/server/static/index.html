          Why this is not the same as SEO

          The bots have split in two

          OpenAI runs three separate crawlers. So does Anthropic. One collects training data; another builds the index the assistant answers from; a third fetches a page when a user asks for it by name. They take separate robots.txt tokens and they do completely different jobs.

          Blocking the wrong one is expensive

          Refusing a training crawler costs you nothing. Refusing a search crawler removes you from AI answers entirely. The names are one hyphen apart, so sites that meant to opt out of training routinely delete themselves from ChatGPT instead — and never find out.

          Your browser will never show you

          A page that renders in JavaScript looks perfect to you and arrives empty at every crawler that does not run it. A CDN rule can refuse AI user-agents while your robots.txt says they are welcome. Both failures are silent, and both are visible from outside in seconds.

          Sightline runs deterministic checks against published crawler documentation. It reports what your configuration does — it does not guess at rankings.
